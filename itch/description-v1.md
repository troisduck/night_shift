# Night Shift

The village runs on the plant. The lights in the windows, the heat in the radiators, all of it comes from the reactor at the edge of town. The people who started it up are past sixty and about to retire. You are the only one from around here who went away and studied nuclear physics. Tonight is your first night shift.

Night Shift is a small simulator of a VVER reactor control room, made for people who have never seen one. You don't need an engineering degree. Palych, the shift supervisor, sits next to you and explains things as they come up. In about twenty minutes you'll know why a reactor can't simply be switched back on.

## What you do

- Raise the control rods a little and wait. Power lags behind your hand, and the "rate" gauge moves first. Impatience trips the reactor.
- Start the pumps before the water gets too hot to carry the heat away.
- Open the turbine valve and give the town 600 MW. Through the control room window you can watch the houses light up.
- Keep the pressure in the green with heaters and spray while the dispatcher phones in a new load.
- Lose a pump at 90% power. Lose the grid in a thunderstorm and start the diesels before the residual heat gets out of hand.
- Restart after a scram and learn about the xenon pit the hard way.
- Then a whole night, 20:00 to 08:00, squeezed into a few minutes.

Nine shifts. A tenth one opens when you've finished the rest.

The control room sounds like a control room. Alarms keep ringing until you acknowledge them. Pumps hum, relays click, the rod drives grind. Headphones help.

There is a key under a lead seal on the scram panel. It disables the reactor protection. Palych will say he never saw you break the seal. You can blow up the reactor from the very first shift, and there is more than one way to do it.

This is a game, not a training course. The physics is simplified and time runs fast, sometimes very fast. But cause and effect hold: heat has to go somewhere, water boils when the pressure drops, and the automatic protection is there for a reason.

## Details

- Language: Russian only for now
- Runs in the browser. One HTML file, works offline
- Keyboard or mouse
- About 15–20 minutes to get the basics, longer to run every shift clean
